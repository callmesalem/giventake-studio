# 05 — Retention

**The point:** project revenue restarts at zero every month. Retainer revenue doesn't.
`../../business/05-pricing-and-positioning.md` §4 makes the case — the Dedicated tier
is the actual business and the projects are the on-ramp.

This folder is how a delivered project becomes a continuing relationship, and how you
notice a client going quiet before they leave rather than after.

---

## Health scoring

Scored from observable behaviour, not sentiment. Weekly, automatic.

| Signal | Healthy | Watch | At risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reply latency | < 2 business days | 2–5 | > 5 |
| Weekly demo attendance | Attends | Misses one | Misses two consecutively |
| Invoice payment | On time | Late once | Late twice, or past day 14 |
| Support tone | Neutral or positive | Terse | Frustration expressed |
| Scope conversations | Normal | Frequent pushback | Disputes what was agreed |
| Decision-maker | Engaged | Delegating away | Unreachable |

**Two "watch" or one "at risk" → escalate to a human.** Not to an automated save
sequence — to a person, who calls.

**DECISION RULE:** health scoring never triggers client-facing contact by itself. It
produces an internal flag. A client who is drifting and receives an automated "we
noticed you seem disengaged!" email will leave faster.

## Cadence after handoff

| When | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Handoff day | Testimonial + review request | **Human asks**, agent tracks |
| Day 14 | Retrospective — **re-measure the baseline** | Human |
| Day 30 | Check-in: is it working as expected? | Agent drafts → human sends |
| Day 60 | Value check + retainer conversation if warranted | Human |
| Day 90 | Referral ask | Human |
| Quarterly | Light touch: something useful, not a pitch | Agent drafts → human sends |

## SOP 05.1 — Retainer conversion

**TRIGGER** — Any of: three or more support tickets in the window · client asks about
ongoing work · day-60 check-in · a second project enquiry from the same client.

**STEPS**
1. Assemble evidence: what they've asked for since handoff, the pattern, what a
   retainer would have covered.
2. Draft the proposal — capacity, monthly fee, shared roadmap, weekly demos, 30-day
   cancellation.
3. **Human reviews, prices, and sends.** Agents never set the number (charter §3.1).

**DECISION RULES** — Conversation is triggered by demonstrated need, never by a
calendar date alone. Selling a retainer to a client who doesn't need one produces a
cancellation in month three and a worse relationship than not selling it.

**VERIFY (CPA)** — how the retainer is invoiced has direct sales-tax consequences.
A bundled retainer is the worst case for apportionment
(`../../business/02-ohio-tax.md` §1). Structure the invoice before selling the first one.

## SOP 05.2 — At-risk playbook

**TRIGGER** — Health score hits the escalation threshold, or dissatisfaction is
expressed anywhere.

**STEPS** — Agent stops all automated contact to that client, assembles a timeline of
recent touchpoints, tickets, and invoices, and notifies the human. **The human calls.**

**PROHIBITIONS** — Agents send nothing to an at-risk client. No check-in, no survey, no
offer. Every automated message to someone already unhappy confirms they're a row in a
system.

### Pre-approved save bands

So a human can act quickly without inventing terms, and so an agent knows the edge:

| Band | Human may offer without further thought | Requires real deliberation |
|---|---|---|
| Goodwill | Extend support window up to 30 days | Anything longer |
| Scope | Absorb one small in-scope fix | Anything with material hours |
| Commercial | Pause a retainer for one month | Discounts, refunds, credits |

**Agents may never offer anything in this table** (charter §3.4). It exists to give the
human a fast decision, not to give an operator a lever.

## SOP 05.3 — Testimonial, review, and referral

The asset the studio most needs, and the entire route back to a real case-studies
section on the site.

**Handoff day — testimonial.** Asked at the moment of delivery, when they're happiest.
Waiting three months means asking someone whose glow has faded.

**Handoff day — Google review.** With a faceless brand, reviews are the main trust
substitute (`../../business/08-marketing-and-client-acquisition.md` §8). Ask directly,
send the link, ask once.

**Day 14 — the case study.** Re-measure the baseline from kickoff
(`projects.baseline_json`) and compare. **Publication requires written permission** —
MSA §4.6 provides for it, so it's a confirmation, not a negotiation.

> This is the whole reason the baseline gets measured at kickoff. The site currently
> has no case studies because there was nothing true to publish. This SOP is how that
> changes — with real numbers, measured, with permission.

**Day 90 — referral.** "Who else do you know with this problem?" is easier to answer
than "do you know anyone who needs a developer." Ask a person, not a category.

**PROHIBITIONS** — Agents never draft a testimonial for a client to approve. A
testimonial written by us and rubber-stamped is a fabricated endorsement, which is the
exact thing the FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule prohibits and the exact
category of problem that forced the site rewrite.

## SOP 05.4 — Offboarding

**TRIGGER** — Retainer cancelled, or engagement ends with no follow-on.

**STEPS** — Confirm the handoff package is complete
(`../../templates/handoff-checklist.md`), confirm access removal in writing, send a
genuine thank-you, ask what could have been better, keep the record warm for win-back.

**Do this properly even when it ends badly.** A client who leaves cleanly still refers
you. One who leaves feeling abandoned tells people. The cost difference is one email.

---

## SOP 05.5 — Win-back

**TRIGGER** — 6 months after a lapsed client, once.

**STEPS** — Human decides whether to contact at all. If yes, agent drafts something
specific to their business — a relevant change, a genuinely useful observation. Human
reviews and sends. **One attempt. No sequence.**

**PROHIBITIONS** — Never a generic "we miss you" campaign. Never more than once a year.
