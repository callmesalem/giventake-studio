## The short version

We build software using AI coding agents, and a human reviews everything before
it ships. That's how we deliver in weeks what traditionally takes months. This
document explains exactly what that means for you — including the parts that are
genuinely less certain than a traditional development shop would tell you.

---

## What the AI does

AI coding agents write most of the code — the scaffolding, the boilerplate, the
repetitive work, the first draft of tests. This is the part of software
development that is time-consuming without being difficult, and it is what used
to make projects take months.

## What a human does

Everything that determines whether the software actually works for your business:

- **Architecture and technical decisions** — how the system is structured, what
  it's built on
- **Reviewing every line before it ships** — AI-generated code is read, not
  skimmed
- **Line-by-line review of the risky parts** — anything touching login,
  permissions, or payments gets manual scrutiny, because that's where automated
  output is most confidently wrong
- **Security review**
- **Testing against the real requirements**, not just whether the code runs
- **Deciding what ships**
- **Talking to you** — you deal with the person building your software, not an
  account manager

**Nothing is deployed without human approval.**

## What we don't let the AI decide

- Your architecture or technology stack
- Your user experience
- What's secure enough to ship
- What "done" means
- Anything we'd have to explain to you afterward

## What this means for you

**Speed.** Weeks instead of months, because the slow mechanical part is
automated.

**Cost.** You're paying for judgment and review rather than for someone typing
boilerplate.

**Quality.** Automated tests on the paths that matter, plus human review. Faster
does not mean less tested — if anything, AI makes comprehensive test coverage
cheaper, so there tends to be more of it.

**Transparency.** You always know how your software was built. It's in your
contract.

## Two things we'll tell you that others might not

We'd rather you hear these from us than from your lawyer after signing.

### 1. Copyright in AI-generated code is legally unsettled

The U.S. Copyright Office's position is that material generated purely by AI
isn't protected by copyright, because copyright requires human authorship. The
law here is still developing.

**What this means practically:** we assign you every right we hold in your
software, and you get complete freedom to use, modify, sell, and build on it.
What we won't do is promise that an exclusive copyright exists in the
AI-generated portions, because that may not be legally true — and a promise we
can't back is worth nothing to you.

**Does this matter for your business?** For almost every business application,
no. What you need is the right to use your software, change it, and stop anyone
else from taking it — and you have all of that. It would matter if your business
model depended on suing someone for copying your source code, which is unusual.
We'd rather flag it than have you discover it later.

### 2. AI tools can reproduce existing code

AI coding assistants learn from public code, and they can occasionally reproduce
parts of it — including code under licences that would impose obligations on your
business if it ended up in your product.

**What we do about it:**

- Run automated licence and provenance scans before delivery
- Provide a software bill of materials listing every component and its licence
- Configure our tools to suppress suggestions matching public code where that
  setting exists
- Flag anything that needs your attention **before** delivery, not after

**What we won't claim:** that this is a perfect guarantee. No scan catches
everything, and this risk exists in traditional development too — developers have
always copied from Stack Overflow. AI makes it more likely and more invisible,
which is why we scan rather than assume.

## Questions clients ask

**"If AI writes the code, why am I paying you?"**
Because the code is the easy part. Knowing what to build, structuring it so it
still works in two years, catching what the AI got confidently wrong, and being
accountable when something breaks — that's the work. The AI made the typing
faster; it didn't make the judgment unnecessary.

**"Is AI-built software lower quality?"**
It's as good as the review. Unreviewed AI code is genuinely bad, and there are
studios shipping exactly that. Reviewed AI code, with tests, is comparable to
hand-written code and usually better tested, because writing tests got cheap.
This is why our review process is in the contract rather than just on the website.

**"What if I don't want AI used on my project?"**
Tell us. We can discuss traditional development, though the timeline and cost
would be closer to conventional agency rates — that difference is exactly what
AI-assisted delivery buys you.

**"Who owns the software?"**
You do, on full payment. See section 4 of the MSA and the copyright note above.

**"What if you disappear?"**
You get the source code, the repository, documentation, and a deployment you
control. Nothing is locked to us. That's true from the first delivery, not just
at the end.
